# AI Capex Diagnostic Modeling

## Objective
Diagnose and correct structural failures in an OLS regression model predicting AI software revenue from 2026 Nvidia AI capital expenditure and deployment data, with a focus on identifying heteroscedasticity-induced inferential distortions and applying HC3-robust estimation to recover valid standard errors.

---

## Methodology

- **Data Preparation:** Ingested and cleaned 2026 Nvidia AI capital expenditure and deployment data using `pandas`, structuring the dataset around capital expenditure tiers as the primary explanatory dimension.
- **Baseline OLS Estimation:** Estimated a naive Ordinary Least Squares model via `statsmodels` to establish a baseline fit and generate initial coefficient estimates, residuals, and p-values.
- **Heteroscedasticity Diagnosis:** Conducted residual analysis using `matplotlib` and `seaborn` to visualize the error structure across fitted values; identified a systematic expansion of residual variance at higher capital expenditure tiers, consistent with multiplicative heteroscedasticity.
- **Multicollinearity Assessment:** Computed Variance Inflation Factors (VIF) across all regressors to detect collinear relationships among deployment metrics, flagging variables with structurally inflated VIF scores.
- **HC3 Robust Correction:** Re-estimated the model using Heteroscedasticity-Consistent (HC3) standard errors to produce asymptotically valid inference without altering the point estimates, directly correcting the false statistical confidence generated by the naive model.
- **Comparative Inference Analysis:** Benchmarked naive vs. robust p-values and confidence intervals side-by-side to isolate which deployment metrics were genuinely significant versus artifacts of understated standard errors.

---

## Key Findings

The naive OLS model produced artificially compressed standard errors across high-capital-expenditure observations, yielding p-values that overstated the statistical significance of several deployment metrics. Residual diagnostics confirmed severe heteroscedasticity with a clear fan structure expanding at upper expenditure tiers — a pattern consistent with variance scaling proportionally to the regressor magnitude.

Correcting with HC3 estimators appropriately widened the standard errors, materially shifting inference: variables that appeared significant under naive OLS failed to maintain significance once robust estimation was applied. This confirms that the naive model's confidence was structurally false — a direct consequence of violated homoscedasticity assumptions rather than genuine signal in the data.

The analysis underscores a critical applied econometrics principle: in high-variance, capital-intensive datasets, failing to correct for heteroscedasticity does not bias coefficient estimates but critically distorts inference, leading to overconfident conclusions about predictor significance.
